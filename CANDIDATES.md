# The Best Advice I Ever Got — Candidates

Competition: `u-pangg7-the-best-advice-i-ever-got-358cefb0`
Gate: one sentence, 1–200 characters, UTF-8, not only whitespace.
Judge (pairwise, in order): (1) one sentence about the best advice the
writer ever received; (2) advice worth carrying — specific, earned, true
beyond the moment; (3) a real voice recalling a real moment, not a slogan
or fortune cookie; (4) tight, every word earns its place, reads in one
breath; (5) lands — the reader keeps something after the last word.

## Candidates

1. (178 chars) My father pressed his last twenty into my hand the morning I left for the city and said, "Spend it on food, not pride — hunger passes, but a debt to a stranger follows you home."
2. (134 chars) When I lost my first job, my mother said, "A closed door is not a wall — knock on the next one before you sit down to mourn this one."
3. (135 chars) My grandfather, fixing a broken fence in the rain, told me, "Do the small repair today, because tomorrow it costs you the whole fence."
4. (140 chars) The night before my first exam, my teacher said, "Write what you know first — a blank page scares you, but a half-full one argues with you."
5. (125 chars) My mother, watching me quit after one failure, said, "You don't have to win today — you only have to show up again tomorrow."
6. (139 chars) When I wanted to borrow money for a shortcut, my uncle said, "If you can't afford the slow road, you definitely can't afford the fast one."
7. (121 chars) My father, after I lied to save face, said, "A lie is a loan with interest — you will pay it back every day you keep it."
8. (152 chars) On the day I got my first paycheck, my grandmother said, "Save the first tenth before you spend a cent — the money you never see is the money you keep."
9. (145 chars) When I complained my boss was unfair, my mentor said, "Don't argue with the weather — build a roof, then decide if you still want to live there."
10. (151 chars) My mother, the morning I left home, said, "Call when you arrive, not when you need something — I worry either way, but only one of them lets me sleep."

## Picked: #1

Why it wins against the judge's criteria:

- **Real moment, real person:** a father handing over his last twenty on
  the morning his child leaves for the city — a scene you can see, with a
  person behind the words, not a slogan floating in the void.
- **Advice worth carrying:** "Spend it on food, not pride" is concrete
  and testable, and it stays true far beyond that morning — pride is the
  expensive habit; hunger is temporary.
- **It lands:** the second half flips the sentence — "a debt to a stranger
  follows you home" — turning a small money tip into a rule for life, and
  the reader keeps that image after the last word.
- **Tight:** 178 characters, one breath, no filler word; the em-dash turn
  does the work of a whole paragraph.

The others were cut because: #2, #5 and #9 lean on familiar metaphors
(door, showing up, weather) that read closer to slogans; #3 and #8 are
solid but their payoff is a proverb rather than a lived consequence;
#4 and #6 are clever but the voice is a teacher/uncle lecturing, not a
moment remembered; #7 is strong but "loan with interest" is a metaphor
about the advice itself, not a consequence lived; #10 is warm but its
landing is about the mother's sleep, not the advice carried forward.
